The Comparison Letters · II

Their outside, your inside

3 min read · by ParaDox

Comparison feels like it's about two people. It isn't. It's about two wildly different quantities of information, held up next to each other as though they're the same kind of thing.

Here's the first scene, and you've lived it this week. You're scrolling, and you see an output. The launch. The trip. The relationship that looks settled. What you're seeing is the last frame of a process — and you're seeing it at the exact moment it was most presentable, chosen from many moments, by someone who wanted it to look like that. You don't see the three attempts that didn't work. You don't see the argument in the car on the way. You don't see the part where they wondered if any of it was worth it. Not because they're deceiving you. Because those aren't the frames anyone posts.

Now the second scene, which is the one that should end the argument. Someone tells you they envy something about your life. And your immediate, involuntary reaction is a kind of laugh — because you know what that thing cost, and what it's actually like from the inside, and how much of it is held together with tape. You are, in that moment, the person on the other side of the asymmetry. You just can't feel it from there.

Here's the tell: the comparison only ever runs in one direction. You compare your unedited interior — every doubt, every 3am, every thing you've never said out loud — against their edited exterior. And you do it instantly, without noticing you've done anything, because both things arrive labelled as a life.

What matters is that this isn't a flaw in your character or a failure of confidence. It's structural. You will never have access to anyone's interior. Not because people hide — most people aren't hiding much — but because interiors aren't transmissible. There is no format for them. You get one from the inside, for free, in unbearable detail, for your entire life. Everyone else, you get the outside. That ratio never changes no matter how well you know someone.

So the one thing to do this week, and it takes ten minutes: pick three people you compare yourself to and write down, for each, what you cannot see. Not speculation about their secret suffering — that's just a different fiction, and a slightly smug one. Write the honest inventory of your own ignorance. What their financial situation actually is. What they're like on an ordinary Tuesday. What they were doing at four in the afternoon two days ago. Whether they like their work or tolerate it. What they're afraid of. Whether they sleep.

The list is always longer than you expect, and that length is the point. You are not comparing yourself to a person. You are comparing yourself to a very short summary, and losing.

This doesn't happen because you're insecure. It happens because you have exactly one interior and access to a functionally infinite supply of exteriors, several hundred of which you'll scroll past before lunch. The arithmetic was never survivable. Any nervous system fed that ratio produces the same result, and yours is working correctly.

You have one inside and everyone else's outside. That was never going to be a fair comparison — it isn't even a comparison.

— ParaDox
